Can I take pictures or videos?

We have invested in a wonderful photographer to capture every special moment of our day, and we can't wait to share those memories with you all!

You are more than welcome to use your phones and snap your own memories during the celebrations. However, during the symbolic ceremony we ask that phones and cameras are put away, so that our photographer can fully focus on capturing those once-in-a-lifetime moments, and so that you can be fully present in them too.

Getting there

Flights

From New Zealand, you may wish to fly into Frankfurt (FRA) or Paris-Charles de Gaulle (CDG). Both need a train journey of around two hours to reach Strasbourg.

From London, Strasbourg's small airport (SXB), 10 km from the city centre, has just two flights a week from London. Conveniently, one of them is on Thursday 29th April, the day before our civil ceremony.

EuroAirport Basel-Mulhouse-Freiburg (BSL/MLH) and Munich (MUC) are alternatives with slightly longer onward journeys.

Driving & car hire

Driving in Strasbourg is a little more intense than in New Zealand (people love their horns, particularly the buses), but much less chaotic than London or parts of Italy.

The city is very walkable, so a car isn't necessary in Strasbourg, and Champagne is reachable by train.

  • Strasbourg: most car hire is by the main train station. We've used SIXT, who were fantastique and spoke fluent English.
  • Champagne: most rentals are in Reims. We've used Europcar, a short walk from Reims station. SIXT also has a desk at Champagne-Ardenne TGV station, handy if arriving by train from Strasbourg.

Trains to Champagne

High-speed trains from Paris and Strasbourg can get you to Champagne in as little as two hours, at up to 320 km/h. Please make your way to Châlons-en-Champagne, where the shuttles depart and where we recommend you stay for the weekend. Book ahead on Trainline, Omio or directly with SNCF Connect.

Things to do

As many of our guests will be travelling from as far as 19,000 km away, we understand you may want to extend your trip beyond the wedding. Alongside the events we're hosting, here is a curated list of other things you may enjoy during your visit.

Nestled on the border of France and Germany with the Rhine running through it, Strasbourg has a rich, blended heritage, from the charming half-timbered houses lining the canals of the Grande Île, to menus filled with Alsatian classics like tarte flambée and local Riesling and natural wines. Strasbourg is also known as the ‘Capital of Christmas’ and is home to the Christkindelsmärik, one of Europe's oldest and largest Christmas markets.

By train

Le Petit Train de Strasbourg

Departs from two spots outside the Cathédrale Notre-Dame. Runs 35–45 minutes with commentary in many languages including French, English and Italian.

€9 adults · €7 children · under 5s free

Batorama sightseeing cruise

Explore Strasbourg, including the European Parliament, on routes lasting 45 minutes to 2 hours. You may wish to hop off at the Parliament for a visit, and return later by boat for only €4.50.

€12.50–€19.30 adults · €0.50 under 4s

See democracy in action

Visit the European Parliament

Visits are free, but reservations are essential and you must bring ID (passports are accepted, NZ driver's licences are not). Based on 2026 dates, the Parliament may well be in session during your visit, and visitors can sit in the gallery during plenary sessions (30 minutes). Multimedia and guided visits are available outside plenary sessions, in French and English.
